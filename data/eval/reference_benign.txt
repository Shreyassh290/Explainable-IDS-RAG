Benign traffic is ordinary activity that matches the expected behavior of the network and its applications. The classifier output in this case is Benign with very high confidence, so no attack is indicated and no escalation is needed. The flow features that carry the decision should be read against the profile of normal activity: values close to the typical range are consistent with legitimate use. A normal flow usually exchanges packets in both directions, completes its connection in the usual way, and has packet sizes and timing that fit the application in use.

NIST SP 800-94 explains that anomaly-based detection compares definitions of normal activity with observed events and flags significant deviations, using profiles built by monitoring typical activity over a period of time. Such profiles can include malicious activity by mistake, may not capture real-world complexity, and generate many false positives, so a benign verdict supported by a profile should still be reviewed periodically. Whitelists of known benign entities are used to reduce false positives for trusted hosts, and thresholds that separate normal from abnormal behavior should be tuned to the environment.

NIST SP 800-61 recommends that organizations profile networks and systems, because profiling measures the characteristics of expected activity levels so that changes in patterns are easier to identify. Team members who understand normal behavior can recognize abnormal behavior more easily, and the best way to gain that knowledge is to review log entries and security alerts regularly. Organizations should also require a baseline level of logging and auditing on all systems, create a log retention policy, keep host clocks synchronized, correlate events across sources, and maintain a knowledge base for quick reference during analysis.
