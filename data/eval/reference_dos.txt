A denial of service attack aims to degrade or block the availability of a service for its legitimate users, and it can be carried out from a single system. In MITRE ATT&CK, a network denial of service (T1498) works by exhausting the bandwidth that a service relies on, while an endpoint denial of service (T1499) denies availability without saturating the network, by exhausting the resources of the host, the operating system, the server application or the web application, or by exploiting the system to cause a persistent crash. A direct network flood (T1498.001) sends a high volume of packets towards the target, and almost any protocol can be used; UDP and ICMP are common, but TCP can be used as well. Adversaries may use the real source address of the attacking system or spoof it, which makes the traffic harder to trace and weakens filtering by source address.

At the flow level, a denial of service is consistent with a very high packet or byte rate, very short or highly regular inter-arrival times, flows that receive little or no reply, and small packets without payload. These features are indicators and have to be read together with their observed values.

NIST SP 800-61 treats such an event as an incident that is prioritized by its functional impact, its information impact and the recoverability from it. The first step is to confirm the signs of the incident from several sources, such as intrusion detection and prevention systems and logs, and to document the evidence. Containment should limit the impact on the service. Boundary filtering can block the source addresses that produce the attack, block the ports that are targeted, or block the protocols used for transport. When the flood exceeds the capacity of the network connection, the traffic has to be filtered upstream by the Internet service provider, by a content delivery network, or by a provider that specializes in denial of service mitigation. Rate limiting and traffic shaping reduce the load on the server, and SYN cookies defend against SYN floods. Identifying the attacking host can take a long time and should not delay containment, eradication and recovery.

Detection improves by looking for flooding tools such as hping3 or nping that send large volumes of packets across multiple ports or addresses. Organizations should prepare a disaster recovery and business continuity plan for exposed services, keep logs with synchronized clocks for later analysis, hold a lessons learned meeting after a major incident, and share technical indicators with partner organizations where appropriate.
